# Security basics

Owner: Ines Carvalho (founder). Last reviewed on 21 September 2026.

These are the rules every member of the team follows, every day. They are short on purpose. If one of them gets in the way of your work, say so in the lobby and we will change the rule instead of working around it.

## Accounts and passwords

- Use the team password manager for every work account. Do not reuse a password.
- The master passphrase for your password manager must be at least **16 characters** long.
- Turn on two-factor sign-in for every work account. Prefer an authenticator app or a security key over text messages.

## Laptops and phones

- Full-disk encryption stays on, and the screen locks after 5 minutes of inactivity.
- Install operating system updates within a week of their release.
- If a work laptop or phone is lost or stolen, tell Ines and Owen **within 2 hours** of noticing. We then lock or wipe the device remotely and change the passwords it could reach.

## Secrets and keys

- Never paste a key, token or password into chat, a ticket or a doc. Secrets live in the cloud secret manager and nowhere else.
- Rotate API keys **every 90 days**, and immediately if you think one was exposed. Ticket #14 tracks moving the last shared keys into the secret manager.
- Test accounts and demos use synthetic data only.

## Customer data

Inspection photos, signatures and job details belong to our customers. Do not copy them to a personal drive, a personal chat or an outside tool. If you need realistic data to reproduce a bug, ask Owen for an anonymised copy.

## Working with AI colleagues in the studio

AI colleagues read what the team has written down and propose things; people decide. Do not paste customer data or secrets into a question, because the question is stored in the room's chat. Treat an answer as a draft until you have opened the page or ticket it cites.

## Reporting a problem

Forward anything that looks like phishing to Ines. Reports are welcome and never blamed, even when you clicked first.

## Access reviews

Owen reviews who has access to what at the end of each quarter, and removes access that nobody has used.
